# Design notes — 555 PWM high-side fan controller (MK4 Golf)

## 1. Topology

High-side **P-channel** switching of a 12 V fan, PWM generated by a ground-referenced **TLC555**, with a **TC4420 gate driver (option B)** as the default gate drive. A level-shift NPN (`Q_lvl`) inverts the 555 output into the TC4420 input; `R_pu` holds the gate at the rail for fail-safe OFF; the TC4420 sinks the gate low for ON.

P-channel high-side was chosen over N-channel + bootstrap because it **holds 100 % duty indefinitely** (needed for the full-speed mode) without a charge pump. A bootstrap N-channel driver (e.g. IR2117) would collapse the gate supply at 100 % duty.

```
+12V ──┬── S [Q1..Q4 P-ch 40V ~3mΩ] D ──┬── FAN+
   bulk caps        gates ◄ driver        ├ flyback (SK54) ┐
       │                                  ├ RC snubber      │
      GND ═══════════════════════════════ FAN- ════════════┘ GND
```

## 2. Why 40 V FETs and 4 in parallel (heatsink-free)

Target: no heatsink at automotive ambient (up to ~85 °C).

- Load current: 300 W / 12 V ≈ **25 A**.
- Split across 4 FETs → **6.25 A each**.
- Conduction loss per FET at RDS(on) ≈ 3 mΩ (40 V class, e.g. SiR464DP):
  `P = I² × R = 6.25² × 0.003 ≈ 0.12 W` per device.
- DPAK θ_JA ≈ 80–100 °C/W (free air, with copper pour). At 0.12 W → only ~10–12 °C rise.
- Junction ≈ 85 + 12 = **~97 °C** worst case — comfortably below 150 °C **without a heatsink**. ✅

40 V (vs 30 V) gives meaningful headroom for the clamped load-dump rail while keeping RDS(on) low. 55 V parts (e.g. IRF4905, 20 mΩ) would dissipate ~0.8 W each and realistically still want a heatsink — hence the switch.

### Paralleling notes
- Add a small **individual gate resistor (e.g. 4.7–10 Ω)** per FET to damp inter-FET gate oscillation and balance switching.
- P-channel FETs share **static** current well (positive RDS(on) tempco). Keep layout symmetric so dynamic sharing during edges is even.

## 3. Switching frequency

`f ≈ 1.44 / ((R1 + RV1) × Ct)`. With R1 ≈ 1 k, RV1 portion and Ct = 10 nF → **~25 kHz**.

- Above audible (no fan whine).
- Low enough that discrete-BJT gate-drive switching losses stay small.
- Diode steering (Da/Db across the pot) decouples duty from frequency for a true **0–100 %** sweep.

## 4. Gate drive

The **TC4420 (U_drv, 6 A)** is the default gate driver using **option B** for the high-side P-channel FETs:

- **OFF (fail-safe):** `R_pu` (4.7 kΩ) pulls the common `GDRV` node up to `VRAIL`, holding Vgs = 0 V. `R_gs` (10 kΩ) holds Vgs→0 if drive is completely lost. FETs OFF by default with no active drive required.
- **ON:** The TC4420 actively **sinks `GDRV` low** (to near GND), making Vgs ≈ −12 V and fully enhancing the P-FETs. The 6 A peak drive switches the combined gate charge of 4× FETs cleanly.
- **Input conditioning:** `Q_lvl` (MMBT3904 NPN) inverts the ground-referenced 555 output into the TC4420 input domain. 555 HIGH → Q_lvl ON → TC4420 IN goes LOW → TC4420 sinks GDRV → P-FETs ON. A 10 kΩ pull-up (`R_pdi`) on the Q_lvl collector defines the HIGH state when the 555 output is low.

A plain ground-referenced driver without option B cannot pull the gate up to the rail for OFF; `R_pu` solves this while the TC4420 handles the active-low drive for ON.

- **Gate Zener clamp (12 V, `Z_g`):** limits Vgs within the ±20 V rating during transients — **gate protection**.
- **`R_gs` (10 kΩ):** pulls gates to source so FETs are **OFF by default** if drive is lost — fail-safe.

The discrete push-pull totem pole (`Q_p`/`Q_n` PNP+NPN pair) is retained as a **DNF (do-not-fit) alternate** — a builder may populate it instead of the TC4420 if the IC is unavailable, but it requires care with base resistors and dead-time to avoid shoot-through.

## 5. Protection stack

| Stage | Part | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fuse | 30 A blade | Short / overcurrent |
| Reverse battery | LM74700-Q1 ideal diode + N-FET | Near-lossless vs 25 W in a Schottky at 25 A |
| Load dump | 5KP24A TVS (+ series choke L1) | ISO 7637-2 transients can exceed 40 V; clamp protects 40 V FETs |
| Input filter | 470 µF 35 V + 1 µF | Stiffen the rail at the input |
| Bulk | 4× 2200 µF 25 V low-ESR | Supply the 25 A switching pulses, ripple current |
| Flyback | SK54 Schottky (drain→fan) + motor diode | Catch inductive kick each PWM cycle and on stop/start |
| Snubber | 10 Ω + 100 nF on drain | Damp Vds ringing, reduce EMI |
| Gate clamp | 12 V Zener | Vgs protection |
| Gate pulldown | 10 kΩ | Fail-safe off |

## 6. Inrush

Charging 4× 2200 µF plus a cold/stalled motor is a large surge. Mitigation:
- **NTC thermistor** (e.g. Ametherm SL22 2R5, 20 A) in series limits the initial surge.
- **Bypass** the NTC with a relay or MOSFET after ~200 ms so it doesn't dissipate continuously.
- Optional: soft-start by ramping PWM duty from 0 over ~500 ms via the RESET/CONTROL pin.

## 7. Layout guidance

- Keep the **power loop tight**: C_bulk → FET source/drain → flyback → GND. Wide copper / pours.
- **Star-ground** the 555 separately from the high-current return to avoid PWM jitter.
- Place the **gate Zener and R_gs close to the FET gates**.
- Thermal: large copper pours under the DPAK tabs act as the heatsink — maximise area and use thermal vias to a back-side pour.
- Place C_byp (100 nF) right at 555 pin 8.

## 8. Open items to validate before build

1. **Load-dump worst case** for your alternator — if unsuppressed dumps exceed the clamp, consider 60 V FETs or a beefier central suppressor.
2. **Fan stall current** — confirm and re-check fuse / NTC / flyback ratings against it.
3. **Gate-drive edges** — TC4420 is the default driver (option B); verify Q_lvl inversion and gate waveform edges at full load before build.
4. **EMI** — automotive CISPR 25; the snubber + tight layout help, may need input filtering.
